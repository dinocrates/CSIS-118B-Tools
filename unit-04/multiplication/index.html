Multiplication register-pair lab

Compare unsigned MUL and signed IMUL — one-operand full products, and truncated two-/three-operand IMUL.

How to use this tool

  • One-operand MUL/IMUL always produces a full double-width product split across a register pair (e.g. DX:AX). A low-width overflow flag does not mean the full product is wrong.
  • Two- and three-operand IMUL are signed-only, keep only the destination-width (truncated) result, and are not offered at 8-bit width in this model.
  • SF, ZF, AF, and PF are undefined after MUL/IMUL — not zero, not stale. This tool marks them explicitly rather than guessing.

Why CF/OF do not mean "the full product is wrong"

Byte signed 48 × 4: the full 16-bit product (AX = 0x00C0 = 192) is correct either way. CF/OF = 1 only reports that the low 8 bits alone, read as a signed AL, cannot represent 192 (it reads as −64). The full double-width product is still exactly right.
